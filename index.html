About this object

This alabaster lamassu — a protective spirit with a human head, the body of a bull or lion, and eagle's wings — once flanked a doorway in the throne room of Ashurnasirpal II's Northwest Palace at Nimrud. Carved in relief from a single slab, it was designed to be seen from the front or the side: look closely at the legs and you'll find five of them, an Assyrian solution that makes the figure read as standing still from the front and striding forward from the side. A cuneiform inscription runs across the surface, part of a standard royal text that Ashurnasirpal had repeated throughout his palace.
